## Summary

FieldNote turns a landscaping crew's photos and hours into a same-evening invoice for small Polish owners. The customer, the pain and the first channel are concrete, and the founder has lived the business. The open points are whether owners pay for software at all, how big the national market is, and who builds the app at eight hours a week.

**Riskiest assumption.** Owners of 2 to 8 person crews will pay 29 EUR a month for faster, photo-backed invoicing instead of keeping the notebook and WhatsApp.

## Cheapest experiments (next two weeks)

1. **Ten calls, three pre-sales** (7 days). Call ten of the forty owners. Describe the same-evening photo invoice and ask for 29 EUR up front for the first month, refundable. Success metric: 3 of 10 pay before anything ships.
2. **Concierge invoice** (14 days). For two owners, do it by hand for two weeks: they send photos and hours on WhatsApp, you send a finished invoice the same evening. Success metric: Both owners ask to continue after two weeks and days-to-payment drops versus their last month.
3. **Software spend check** (7 days). In the same calls, ask what each owner pays today for any app, tool or accountant service. Success metric: At least 6 of 10 already pay for something monthly.

## The idea

> FieldNote: a phone app for owners of small landscaping crews (2 to 8 people) in Poland. The crew lead photographs the site before and after, taps start and stop per job, and the owner gets a ready invoice the same evening with photos attached. Today most owners write hours in a notebook or a WhatsApp group and invoice a week later, which means arguments about what was done and late payments. Price: 29 EUR per month per company, flat. I ran a crew like this for six years and still know about 40 owners in my region. First channel: I will call them, then the regional trade group on Facebook. Stage: idea and a clickable prototype, evenings only, no outside money.

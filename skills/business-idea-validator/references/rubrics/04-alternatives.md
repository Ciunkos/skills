# Rubric 4: Alternatives

**Id:** `alternatives` (core rubric)

**Question:** What do these customers do about the problem today, and why would they switch to this?

## What to look for

- **The status quo.** Doing nothing, a spreadsheet, a notebook, an assistant, a generic tool (Notion, WhatsApp, Excel), a cheaper manual service. The strongest competitor is usually "what they do now".
- **Named competitors.** Does the text know them? Not knowing the three obvious products in the category is a red flag in itself.
- **Switching cost.** Data to migrate, habits to break, people to retrain, contracts to end.
- **The reason to switch.** Ten times cheaper, ten times faster, a result the alternative cannot produce, or a segment the incumbents ignore. "Better UX" and "AI-powered" are rarely enough on their own.
- **Positioning.** Is the difference something the customer would say back in their own words?

## Scoring anchors

- **5, Strong.** The text names the status quo and the main competitors, states a switching reason a customer would recognize, and the switching cost is low or handled.
- **4, Solid.** The status quo is understood and the difference is real, but switching cost is not addressed or competitors are only partly known.
- **3, Unclear.** The difference is stated as a feature list; it is not clear why a customer would move.
- **2, Weak.** The text ignores the status quo ("there is no competition") or the only difference is a technology the incumbents can add in a quarter.
- **1, Red flag.** A well-funded incumbent already does this for the same segment at a price the idea cannot beat, or the switching cost makes a move irrational.

## Confidence

- **High** when the text names alternatives and the answers cover what customers use today, or when general knowledge settles it: the status quo is a free, well-known product (the platform itself, a built-in feature, a spreadsheet) that no extra text from the founder would change. Say which in `reasoning`.
- **Medium** when you filled gaps from category knowledge the founder could still overturn; say so in `assumptions`.
- **Low** when the alternatives are your guess.

## Traps

- "No competition" means either no problem or no research. Score accordingly and say which you suspect. Silence is different: when the text simply does not mention alternatives and general knowledge does not settle them, score 3 with low confidence.
- Regulatory dependency is scored in rubric 10. Mention it here in one sentence only when it changes what the alternative is.
- Compare against what the customer in rubric 2 uses, not against what a tech founder would use.
- A feature advantage is temporary; a distribution or data advantage lasts.
- Free alternatives (a spreadsheet, a free tier) count as competitors.

## Output

In `evidence`, quote what the text says about today's workaround and competitors. In `assumptions`, list the alternatives you added from general knowledge, marked as such. In `questions`, ask what the last three prospects said they use today.

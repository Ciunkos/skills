# Rubric 9: Founder fit

**Id:** `founder`

**Question:** Why this founder, and do they have the skills, time and runway to get to the first proof?

## What to look for

- **Domain insight.** Has the founder lived the problem, sold to this customer, or worked in this industry? Insight the market cannot read in a blog post.
- **Skills on the team.** Can they build it, sell it, and run it? A solo technical founder with a sales-heavy idea, or a solo business founder with a deep-tech idea, has a gap to name.
- **Time and runway.** Full-time or evenings; months of runway; other commitments. The text may not say; the answers often do.
- **Unfair advantage.** An audience, a customer list, a partner, a prior product, a credential the customer respects.
- **Commitment signal.** Has the founder already talked to customers, built a prototype, spent money?

## Scoring anchors

- **5, Strong.** The founder has lived the problem or sold to this customer, the team covers build and sell, and there is runway for at least the validation phase.
- **4, Solid.** Strong on one axis (domain or build) with a clear plan to cover the other.
- **3, Unclear.** The text says nothing about the founder; score 3 and mark confidence low.
- **2, Weak.** A visible gap with no plan (cannot build and has no budget to hire; cannot sell and the idea needs sales), or time that is clearly insufficient.
- **1, Red flag.** The founder is in a position that makes the idea impossible (no access to the required data or license, a conflict with a current employer, no runway at all).

## Confidence

General rule (see `report-style.md`): high when the text settles the score, or when general knowledge settles it in a way no extra text would change; medium when you filled gaps the founder could still overturn; low when you assumed most of it.

- **High** when the answers or text describe the founder's background, time and resources.
- **Medium** when the stage field or hints give part of it.
- **Low** when nothing is said; this is the usual case, do not inflate it.

## Traps

- Do not score enthusiasm. Score access, skills and time.
- Being a developer is not domain insight into landscaping, dentistry or logistics.
- "I will find a co-founder" is a plan only if the text says how.
- Prior startup experience helps but does not replace customer insight.

## Output

Quote what the text says about the founder. In `assumptions`, state what you assumed about the team. In `questions`, ask about domain contact with the customer, hours per week, and runway in months.

# Rubric 2: Customer

**Id:** `customer` (core rubric)

**Question:** Who exactly buys, can the founder reach them, and do they have the budget and authority to pay?

## What to look for

- **Specificity.** "Small businesses" is not a customer. "Owners of 2 to 10 person landscaping crews in Poland who invoice by the hour" is.
- **Buyer vs user.** Who signs, who uses, who blocks. B2B ideas often name the user and forget the buyer.
- **Reachability.** Where these people gather: a community, a trade show, a software they already use, a search query they type. The text should show the founder knows where they are.
- **Budget and authority.** Does this person already spend money on this category? Can they decide alone?
- **Segment size vs focus.** One narrow first segment is a strength. Three segments at once is a weakness at this stage.

## Scoring anchors

- **5, Strong.** A named, narrow first segment with a clear buyer, a known place to reach them, and evidence they already spend money on this category.
- **4, Solid.** Segment and buyer are clear; reach or budget is plausible but not shown.
- **3, Unclear.** A segment is named but broad, or the buyer and user are mixed up, or reach is not addressed.
- **2, Weak.** "Anyone who…" style customer, or a customer that has no budget (students, hobbyists) without a plan for who pays instead.
- **1, Red flag.** No customer named, or the customer named would not pay for this under any pricing (the pain lands on someone else).

## Confidence

General rule (see `report-style.md`): high when the text settles the score, or when general knowledge settles it in a way no extra text would change; medium when you filled gaps the founder could still overturn; low when you assumed most of it.

- **High** when the text or answers name the segment, the buyer and the way to reach them.
- **Medium** when the segment is there but buyer or reach is missing.
- **Low** when you inferred the customer from the product description. A stated non-segment ("everyone", "anyone who") is not an inference: score 1 or 2 with high confidence, because the text settles that no customer was chosen.

## Traps

- Consumers "would love it" is not a customer. Love is not budget.
- Two-sided marketplaces have two customers; score the harder side and say which.
- A customer the founder cannot reach is not a customer yet.
- Beware "we will start with SMBs, then go enterprise": score the first segment only.

## Output

Quote the words that name the customer. If the text names several segments, list them in `assumptions` and say which one you scored. In `questions`, ask who signs the invoice and where ten of them can be found this month.
